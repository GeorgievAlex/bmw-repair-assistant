# Fluids and capacities reference

One place to check what goes where before a parts order, instead of opening five procedure pages. Everything here is approximate and generation-general, confirm against your owner's manual or RealOEM with your VIN before buying, especially capacities, which vary with whether the system was fully drained.

## Engine oil

Spec commonly cited for this engine is BMW Longlife-01, 5W-30. Capacity is roughly 6.5 litres with a filter change.

The N52 is known for using some oil between changes, this is not automatically a fault. What matters is whether consumption is stable or climbing. Check the level regularly rather than assuming it's fine between services.

## Coolant

BMW-specific blue antifreeze for this generation, mixed with distilled water to the ratio in your owner's manual.

Do not substitute a generic universal coolant. The cooling system on this engine is full of plastic components and the wrong chemistry attacks them, which is part of why thermostat housings and expansion tanks fail on these cars. This is one of the places where "it's all just antifreeze" is actively wrong.

## Brake fluid

Check your owner's manual for the required DOT rating. Brake fluid is hygroscopic, it pulls water out of the air whether or not the car gets driven, which lowers its boiling point and corrodes the system internally. This is why it's a time-based change rather than a mileage-based one.

## Power steering fluid

This generation uses a BMW-specified hydraulic fluid, commonly a CHF-type rather than conventional ATF. Verify the exact spec before topping up, the wrong fluid attacks the seals in the rack and pump.

## Transmission and differential

Both the automatic transmission and the rear differential were marketed as lifetime-fill from the factory. A lot of owners of high-mileage examples disagree with that, on the view that "lifetime" means the warranty period rather than the life of the car, and change them anyway.

Whichever side of that you land on, these use specific fluids that are not interchangeable with generic equivalents. Verify the exact spec for your transmission code before touching either.

## Washer fluid

Not worth overthinking, but use one rated for your winter temperatures. Topping up with water in a cold climate cracks the reservoir and pump.

## A note on capacities

Every figure above is a starting point for ordering, not a measurement to fill blindly to. Fill to the level indication on the dipstick, reservoir, or fill plug, not to a number from a reference table. Residual old fluid left in a system means the actual refill volume is almost always less than the dry capacity.

Source: general public automotive reference knowledge, written for this project, not extracted from any manual. Verify specifics for your exact build and year.
