# General maintenance guide

A rough service rhythm for an E90 325i that's past warranty and being maintained by its owner rather than a dealer. BMW's own condition-based service system stretches some intervals further than many owners are comfortable with on an older car, so a lot of people running these at this age shorten them deliberately.

## Every oil change (roughly 8,000-10,000 km, or once a year)

Many owners halve BMW's original recommended interval on an aging N52, on the reasoning that oil is cheap and this engine's known wear points are not. While you're under there with the undertray off, it costs nothing to look at:

- Oil leaks around the valve cover, oil filter housing, and oil pan gasket
- Coolant weeping from the thermostat housing, water pump, or expansion tank seams
- Boots and bushings on the front control arms, cracked rubber is the early warning
- Brake pad thickness and rotor lip through the wheel spokes
- Tyre wear pattern, uneven wear points at alignment or worn suspension before you feel anything in the steering

## Every couple of years

- Brake fluid, it's hygroscopic and absorbs water from the air whether you drive the car or not, which lowers its boiling point and corrodes the system from inside
- Coolant condition check, and a full change on the interval in the coolant procedure
- Cabin and engine air filters, more often if you're somewhere dusty or pollen-heavy
- Battery condition test, especially before winter, and remember registration if you replace it

## Watch items specific to this engine and chassis

These aren't scheduled, they're the things that fail on an N52 E90 with age and are worth knowing before they strand you:

- Electric water pump and plastic thermostat housing, a well-known pair, many owners replace both proactively around the 120,000-160,000 km mark rather than waiting
- Plastic coolant pipes and the expansion tank, they crack from heat cycling
- Valve cover gasket seepage, usually a slow leak rather than a sudden failure
- VANOS solenoids, show up as rough idle or a check engine light
- Front control arm bushings and rear trailing arm bushings, both chassis wear items that change how the car drives long before they're dangerous

## Before winter

- Battery test, cold weather exposes a marginal battery immediately
- Coolant strength check for your climate
- Tyre tread depth and pressures, and the spare if the car has one
- Wiper blades and washer fluid rated for your temperatures

## A note on intervals

The numbers above are a common owner-maintained rhythm, not BMW's official schedule. Your car's condition-based service readings, your climate, and how it's driven all shift them. Check your owner's manual for the official intervals and treat this as a starting point for a conversation with whoever maintains the car, not a replacement for it.
