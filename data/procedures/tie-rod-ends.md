# Tie rod end replacement

## Category
Steering

## Summary
Replace worn outer tie rod ends. Symptoms are play in the steering, a knock when turning at low speed, and uneven inner or outer tyre wear.

## Steps
### Preparation
1. Loosen the wheel bolts, jack the car, support it on stands, remove the front wheel.
2. Before loosening anything, mark the position of the outer tie rod on its threaded shaft, or count the exact number of turns as you remove it. This gets you close to the original toe setting and makes the car safe to drive to the alignment shop.
3. Soak the ball joint nut and the lock nut in penetrating fluid.

### Removal
1. Loosen the lock nut that holds the outer tie rod against the inner rod.
2. Remove the castle nut or locking nut at the steering knuckle.
3. Separate the ball joint with a proper separator tool, not by hammering the knuckle.
4. Unscrew the outer tie rod off the inner rod, counting turns as you go.

### Installation
1. Thread the new outer tie rod on the same number of turns you counted, to land close to the original toe.
2. Insert the ball joint into the knuckle and torque its nut to spec, fitting a new cotter pin if your design uses one.
3. Snug the lock nut against the outer tie rod, but leave the final tightening to after alignment if the shop prefers to set it themselves.
4. Reinstall the wheel, lower the car.
5. Get a four-wheel alignment. This is not optional on a steering component, counting turns gets you drivable, not correct.

## Torque Specs
Tie rod ball joint nut and lock nut: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec. If your design uses a cotter pin, never reuse the old one.

## Part Numbers
Outer tie rod ends, and inner tie rods if those are also worn: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. Left and right can differ.

## Tools
Ball joint separator, torque wrench, penetrating fluid, jack and stands.

## Difficulty
Medium

## Time Estimate
1-1.5 hours per side

## Notes
Check the inner tie rods while you're in there, grab the inner rod and feel for play, there's no point replacing only the outer if the inner is also worn. Counting threads on removal is the single trick that makes this job go smoothly, skip it and you're guessing at toe. Alignment afterward is mandatory, this is a steering component and getting it wrong affects how the car tracks and brakes.
