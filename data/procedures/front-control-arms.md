# Front control arms and bushings

## Category
Suspension

## Summary
Replace the front lower control arms (often called thrust arms and tension struts on this chassis) or just their bushings. One of the most common wear items on an E9X, usually announced by a clunk over bumps and vague steering.

## Steps
### Preparation
1. Loosen the wheel bolts, jack the car up, support it properly on stands, remove the front wheel.
2. Spray the control arm ball joint nuts and bushing bracket bolts with penetrating fluid and give it time to work, these live in the salt spray and seize readily.

### Removal
1. Remove the nut from the ball joint where the arm meets the steering knuckle.
2. Separate the ball joint with a proper ball joint separator, not by hammering the knuckle, you can damage the knuckle or the new part's boot.
3. Unbolt the bushing bracket from the subframe, note bolt positions and lengths before they come out.
4. Remove the arm. If you're only replacing bushings rather than the whole arm, press the old bushing out now, this needs a press or the right puller setup, it's not a hand-tool job.

### Installation
1. Fit the new arm or pressed-in bushing, hand-start all fasteners before torquing anything.
2. Torque the ball joint nut and bushing bracket bolts to spec.
3. Critically: final-torque the bushing bolts with the suspension at normal ride height, not hanging free on the jack. Torquing a rubber bushing at full droop preloads it permanently and it will tear early.
4. Reinstall the wheel, lower the car, and get a four-wheel alignment, this job changes the geometry.

## Torque Specs
Ball joint nut and bushing bracket bolts: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec, these are substantial fasteners and several are one-time-use torque-to-yield on this chassis, check before reusing any of them.

## Part Numbers
Control arms, tension struts, and their bushings: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. Left and right are often different part numbers, and some years changed the bushing design.

## Tools
Ball joint separator, torque wrench, penetrating fluid, jack and stands, and either a press or a bushing puller kit if you're doing bushings rather than complete arms.

## Difficulty
Medium-Hard

## Time Estimate
2-3 hours per side

## Notes
Replacing complete arms rather than pressing bushings is more expensive in parts but far less painful in labor, worth pricing both ways before starting. Always get an alignment afterward, skipping it will eat a set of tires. If one side is worn, the other is usually not far behind, doing both at once saves repeating the whole teardown.
