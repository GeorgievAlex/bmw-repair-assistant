# Sway bar end link replacement

## Category
Suspension

## Summary
Replace worn anti-roll bar (sway bar) end links. Usually the cause of a light rattle or knock over small bumps that sounds much worse than the actual repair is.

## Steps
### Preparation
1. Loosen the wheel bolts, jack the car, support it on stands, remove the wheel.
2. Soak both end link nuts in penetrating fluid, these seize more often than almost anything else on the car.

### Removal
1. Hold the ball stud still while you undo the nut. Most end links have a hex or torx socket in the end of the stud exactly for this, otherwise the whole stud just spins and you'll be there a while.
2. Remove the upper nut (strut or subframe end) and the lower nut (sway bar end).
3. Withdraw the old link. If the stud spins hopelessly and won't come off, cutting the link is a legitimate last resort since it's being replaced anyway.

### Installation
1. Fit the new link, start both nuts by hand.
2. Torque both ends to spec, holding the stud still the same way you did on removal.
3. Reinstall the wheel and lower the car.

## Torque Specs
End link nuts: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec. These are modest fasteners, overtightening damages the ball joint boot and the new link will fail early.

## Part Numbers
Sway bar end links, front and rear: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. Front and rear are different lengths, and some years differ between standard and sport suspension.

## Tools
Penetrating fluid, torque wrench, and whatever hex or torx bit fits the counter-hold in your link's stud, check before you start so you're not stuck mid-job.

## Difficulty
Easy

## Time Estimate
30-45 minutes per side

## Notes
Among the cheapest fixes for a suspension noise, always worth ruling out before chasing more expensive parts. Replace both sides at once, they wear at the same rate and you've already got the car up. No alignment needed afterward, end links don't affect geometry.
