# Rear shock absorber replacement

## Category
Suspension

## Summary
Replace the rear shock absorbers. On this chassis the rear spring and shock are separate components, which makes this a notably easier and safer job than the front struts.

## Steps
### Preparation
1. Loosen the rear wheel bolts, jack the car, support it on stands, remove the wheel.
2. Support the rear trailing arm with a jack so the suspension stays loaded as you work.

### Removal
1. Access the upper shock mount. Depending on body style this may mean pulling back trim in the trunk or under the parcel shelf to reach the top nut.
2. Remove the lower shock bolt at the trailing arm.
3. Remove the upper mount nut and withdraw the shock. The mount hardware and dust boot usually come out with it.

### Installation
1. Fit the new shock with a new mount and bump stop, same reasoning as the front, these are wear items and cheap relative to doing the job twice.
2. Start the upper nut and the lower bolt by hand before torquing either.
3. Torque the lower bolt with the suspension at ride height, not hanging free, so the bushing isn't preloaded into a permanently twisted position.
4. Refit any trim removed for access, reinstall the wheel, lower the car.

## Torque Specs
Upper shock mount nut and lower shock bolt: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec, and check whether yours are one-time-use before planning to reuse them.

## Part Numbers
Rear shocks, upper mounts, and bump stops: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. Sport suspension and standard are different part numbers.

## Tools
Torque wrench, jack and stands, trim removal tools for the upper mount access, penetrating fluid.

## Difficulty
Medium

## Time Estimate
1-1.5 hours per side

## Notes
Much less dangerous than the front struts since the spring stays in the car, no spring compressor needed. Replace in pairs across the axle. If you're also doing the rear trailing arm bushings, do both jobs in the same session, the teardown overlaps significantly.
