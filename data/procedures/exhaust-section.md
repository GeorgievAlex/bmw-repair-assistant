# Exhaust section replacement

## Category
Engine

## Summary
Replace a rusted or blown exhaust section, typically the mid-section or rear silencer. Usually announced by a drone that gets louder under load, or a rasp on cold start that quietens as things expand.

## Steps
### Preparation
1. Work on a cold car. An exhaust at operating temperature will burn you through gloves and there is no version of this job worth rushing for.
2. Raise the car and support it on stands, with enough height to work along its length comfortably.
3. Soak every clamp bolt and flange nut in penetrating fluid and leave it. On a car this age these are the most seized fasteners you will meet, and a second application half an hour later is time well spent.

### Removal
1. Support the section you're removing with a jack or a stand before undoing anything. A freed exhaust section is heavier and more awkward than it looks hanging there.
2. Undo the flange bolts or clamp at each end of the section.
3. Work the rubber hangers off their mounts. A spray of soapy water and a hanger removal tool, or a flat pry bar, makes this far less of a fight than pulling by hand.
4. Lower the section out. If a flange bolt shears rather than turns, which is common, that's normal on rusted hardware, plan for drilling it out or replacing the stud.

### Installation
1. Fit a new gasket at each flange joint, do not reuse the old one, this is where comeback leaks come from.
2. Replace any rubber hangers that are cracked or stretched while you have access, they're cheap and a sagging exhaust rattles against the underbody.
3. Offer the new section up and hand-start all fasteners before tightening any, so the alignment can still settle.
4. Tighten evenly across each flange, working side to side rather than fully torquing one bolt at a time.
5. Start the engine cold and listen along the length of the system for leaks, a flange leak ticks or puffs rather than drones.

## Torque Specs
Flange bolts and clamp hardware: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec. These are modest fasteners and overtightening a flange distorts it, which causes the leak you were trying to fix.

## Part Numbers
Exhaust section, flange gaskets, clamps, and rubber hangers: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. Exhaust configurations vary between engine variants and model years more than people expect, so confirm against your car rather than the model name.

## Tools
Penetrating fluid, jack and stands, a jack or stand to support the section, exhaust hanger removal tool or pry bar, and realistically an angle grinder or drill for at least one seized fastener.

## Difficulty
Medium

## Time Estimate
1.5-3 hours depending on how much is seized

## Notes
Budget for broken hardware rather than being surprised by it, buy the clamps, gaskets and hangers up front so a sheared bolt doesn't end the day. Never work under a car on a jack alone, and be particularly careful here because you're pulling and levering at awkward angles, which is exactly how a poorly supported car gets shifted. If the section you need is only sold as part of a larger assembly, price a reputable independent exhaust shop before committing, section welding is sometimes genuinely the cheaper and better answer.
