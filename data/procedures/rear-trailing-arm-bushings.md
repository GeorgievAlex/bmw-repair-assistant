# Rear trailing arm bushings (RTAB)

## Category
Suspension

## Summary
Replace the rear trailing arm bushings, a well-known wear point on this chassis. Worn RTABs show up as rear-end vagueness, a slight steering sensation from the back of the car under throttle or braking, and uneven rear tyre wear.

## Steps
### Preparation
1. Loosen the rear wheel bolts, jack the car, support it on stands, remove the wheel.
2. Support the trailing arm with a jack, you'll need to control its position throughout.
3. Soak the bushing bracket bolts in penetrating fluid.

### Removal
1. Remove the bolts holding the trailing arm bushing bracket to the body.
2. Lower the trailing arm just enough to access the bushing, without stretching the brake line or ABS wire, disconnect their brackets if needed rather than straining them.
3. Press the old bushing out. This genuinely needs an RTAB removal/installation tool kit, the bushing is a tight interference fit and improvising here usually damages the arm.

### Installation
1. Press the new bushing in with the tool, paying attention to orientation. These bushings are directional and installing one rotated incorrectly ruins its intended compliance.
2. Reattach the bracket to the body, hand-start the bolts.
3. Final-torque with the suspension at ride height, not drooping on the jack. This matters more here than almost anywhere else on the car, a bushing torqued at full droop is permanently preloaded and will tear.
4. Reinstall the wheel, lower the car, get a rear alignment.

## Torque Specs
Trailing arm bushing bracket bolts: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec, and check whether they're one-time-use before planning to reuse them.

## Part Numbers
RTAB bushings, and the limiter kit if you choose to fit one: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. There are also polyurethane and solid monoball aftermarket options, each trading compliance and noise for precision, worth researching before committing since they feel very different on the road.

## Tools
RTAB removal and installation tool kit (rentable from many parts stores), torque wrench, jack and stands, penetrating fluid.

## Difficulty
Hard

## Time Estimate
2-4 hours per side depending on corrosion

## Notes
Bushing orientation is the step people get wrong, check the alignment marks before pressing. Doing this job is a natural time to also do rear shocks since the teardown overlaps. Get a rear alignment afterward, this directly affects rear toe.
