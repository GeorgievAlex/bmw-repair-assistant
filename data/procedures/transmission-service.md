# Transmission fluid and pan service

## Category
Transmission

## Summary
Change the automatic transmission fluid and filter, and replace the pan gasket. This is the transmission job that's genuinely doable on a driveway. Internal repair is not, see the note at the bottom.

## Steps
### Preparation
1. This fluid is filled to a level checked at a specific temperature, not to a dipstick reading, and many of these cars have no dipstick at all. Read the fill procedure for your exact transmission before draining anything, because the refill is the hard part of this job, not the drain.
2. You need the correct fluid for your specific transmission code, confirmed before you start. Transmission fluids are not interchangeable and the wrong one causes shifting faults that look like mechanical failure.
3. Warm the car gently so the fluid flows, then raise it and support it level on stands. Level matters here, a car tilted on a slope gives a false fill level.
4. Remove the undertray.

### Draining
1. Position a wide drain pan, this will come out faster and spread further than engine oil.
2. Remove the drain plug and let it drain fully.
3. Drop the pan. On many of these the filter is integrated into the pan itself, so the pan and filter are one replaceable part rather than two.
4. Expect some residue in the pan. A light grey film is normal wear. Metal flakes, glitter, or anything chunky is not, and means you stop and reassess rather than refill and hope.

### Refilling
1. Fit the new pan and gasket, torque the pan bolts evenly in a crossing pattern.
2. Refill through the fill plug with the correct fluid, using a pump. This is a fill-from-below job, there's no pouring it in from the engine bay.
3. Bring the transmission to the specified check temperature with the engine running, cycling through the gear positions as the procedure for your transmission describes.
4. At that temperature, with the engine still running, open the fill plug and let the excess run out until it drips rather than streams. That drip point *is* the correct level.
5. Fit the fill plug, lower the car, and drive it through a range of gears watching for shifting changes or leaks.

## Torque Specs
Pan bolts, drain plug and fill plug: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec for your exact transmission code. The pan bolts in particular are low torque into an alloy casing and are easy to strip.

## Part Numbers
Pan with integrated filter, pan gasket, drain and fill plug seals, and the correct fluid for your transmission code: verify all of these against RealOEM.com using your VIN. Getting the transmission code right matters more here than anywhere else on this site.

## Tools
Fluid pump for filling from below, wide drain pan, torque wrench, jack and stands, and a way to read transmission temperature (a scan tool that reads the transmission module, not a generic OBD2 code reader).

## Difficulty
Hard

## Time Estimate
2-4 hours

## Notes
BMW marketed this as a lifetime fill. Plenty of owners of high-mileage cars read "lifetime" as "the warranty period" and change it anyway. Both positions are defensible, but there's one real caveat: on a transmission that has gone a very long way on original fluid and is already shifting poorly, a fresh fluid change occasionally makes behaviour worse rather than better. If yours is shifting badly now, get an opinion before changing the fluid, not after.

On internal repair: rebuilding one of these is a specialist bench job needing a clean room, a press, special tools and the clearances for your exact unit. There is no honest driveway version, and a procedure written from general knowledge would be actively dangerous to follow. That's why it isn't on this site. If yours needs internal work, that's a specialist's job.
