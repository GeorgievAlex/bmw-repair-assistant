E90 325i · N52B25

Engine · E90 · N52B25

Valve cover (gasket) replacement

Replace a leaking valve cover gasket, a common age-related leak on this engine, usually shows up as a faint burning smell or visible seepage down the back of the engine.

TorqueThis engine was built with two different valve cover designs across its production run, a plastic cover and a magnesium/aluminum cover, and they have different specs. Plastic cover bolts: commonly cited around 9 Nm. Magnesium cover bolts: commonly cited around 7 Nm plus an additional 90 degree turn, and those bolts are typically a one-time-use, torque-to-yield design, don't reuse them. Confirm which cover type you actually have before using either number, and verify against RealOEM or dealer spec regardless.
PartsValve cover (with integrated gasket, if your design uses one) and cover bolts if they're the one-time-use type: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN, this is one of the areas where getting the wrong variant for your specific cover type is an easy mistake.
ToolsTorque wrench capable of low Nm values and angle-based tightening if your cover uses the torque-to-yield bolts, basic hand tools for the intake/coil components in the way.
Time1.5-2.5 hours depending on what's in the way on your specific car
DifficultyMedium

Steps

Preparation

1. Disconnect the battery negative terminal.

Removal

  1. Remove the engine cover and intake components in the way, on this engine that typically includes some combination of the ignition coil connectors, coil packs themselves, and nearby wiring harness clips.
  2. Disconnect the VANOS solenoid connectors near the front of the cover if they're in the way of cover removal.
  3. Remove the valve cover bolts in the reverse of the tightening sequence below, lift the cover off carefully, the gasket is often integrated into the cover itself on this engine rather than a separate piece.
  4. Clean the cylinder head mating surface thoroughly, any old gasket material or oil residue left behind causes a new leak almost immediately.

Installation

  1. Install the new cover/gasket, hand-thread all bolts first before torquing any of them.
  2. Torque bolts in a crisscross pattern working from the center bolts outward, in stages rather than going straight to full torque on each bolt.
  3. Reconnect everything in reverse, reconnect the battery, start the engine and check for leaks after it's warmed up.

Notes

Identify your cover type (plastic vs magnesium/aluminum) before you start, the wrong torque spec on the wrong bolt type either leaves it loose enough to leak immediately or over-torques a one-time-use bolt. Never reuse torque-to-yield bolts if that's what your cover uses. Take photos of connector routing before you start disconnecting things, there are more clips and connectors in this area than you'll remember afterward.
