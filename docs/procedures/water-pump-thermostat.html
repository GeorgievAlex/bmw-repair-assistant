E90 325i · N52B25

Cooling · E90 · N52B25

Water pump and thermostat replacement

Replace the electric water pump and the thermostat. These are the best-known failure point on this engine family, they fail with age rather than mileage, and the labour overlaps almost completely, so they are nearly always done together.

TorqueWater pump mounting bolts and thermostat housing bolts: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec for your exact revision. These are low-torque fasteners into aluminium, and this is a bad place to guess high.
PartsElectric water pump, thermostat, and the gaskets or O-rings for both: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. There were revised pump versions across the production run, so check whether a superseded part number applies to yours rather than ordering by model name.
ToolsTorque wrench reading accurately at low values, drain pan, BMW-spec coolant, cooling system bleed or vacuum fill tool if you can get one, and diagnostic software if your car needs the pump registered.
Time3-5 hours
DifficultyHard

Steps

Preparation

  1. Engine completely cold. This job opens the cooling system in several places and a warm one will scald you.
  2. Disconnect the battery negative terminal. The pump is electrically driven and you'll be unplugging it.
  3. Raise the car, support it on stands, remove the undertray.
  4. Drain the coolant (see the coolant change procedure). You cannot do this job without dropping the coolant first.

Removal

  1. Remove whatever blocks access on your specific car. Depending on build this can include intake ducting, the fan and shroud, and a belt or pulley in front of the pump.
  2. Unplug the water pump's electrical connector. Note its routing before you free it.
  3. Undo the coolant hoses at the pump and thermostat housing. Have the drain pan positioned, there is always residual coolant regardless of how well it drained.
  4. Remove the pump mounting bolts and withdraw the pump.
  5. Remove the thermostat housing bolts and the housing. If the housing is plastic and original, expect it to be brittle, and expect at least one fastener to fight you.
  6. Clean both mating surfaces properly. Old gasket residue here is a guaranteed comeback leak.

Installation

  1. Fit the new pump with a new gasket or O-ring, and the new thermostat with its own new seal. Never reuse either seal.
  2. Torque the pump and housing bolts evenly in a crossing pattern. These thread into alloy and are easy to strip.
  3. Reconnect hoses and the pump's electrical connector, routed as it was.
  4. Refill with BMW-spec coolant and bleed the system thoroughly. This step decides whether the job worked, see the notes.
  5. Reconnect the battery, run the engine to temperature with the heater on full, and watch the gauge and for leaks at every joint you opened.

After the job

  1. Some cars want the new pump registered or the cooling system adapted via diagnostic software. Check whether yours does before assuming the job is finished.
  2. Recheck the coolant level once fully cold, and again after a few drives. Air works its way out over the first heat cycles and the level will drop.

Notes

This is the job the rest of this site keeps pointing at. If either part is original on a car of this age, doing both together is the normal call, the labour is nearly all shared and coming back in a year for the other one is the worse outcome. The bleed is the step that decides the result. Trapped air in this system produces exactly the overheating you just paid to fix, and people conclude the new pump is faulty when it's air. Take the bleed seriously and recheck the level across several heat cycles. While everything is apart, inspect the plastic coolant pipes and the expansion tank. They crack from the same heat cycling that kills the pump, and you will never have easier access to them than right now.
