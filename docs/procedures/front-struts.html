Suspension · E90 · N52B25

Front strut and spring replacement

Replace the front struts (shock absorber inserts), usually together with the spring, strut mount, and bump stop as a complete assembly.

TorqueUpper strut mount nuts and lower strut-to-knuckle fasteners: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec, several of these are one-time-use on this chassis. Wheel bolts: around 120 Nm.
PartsStruts, springs, strut mounts, and bump stops: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. Spring rates differ between standard, sport, and xDrive setups, ordering the wrong spring changes ride height.
ToolsSpring compressor (only if reusing springs), torque wrench, jack and stands, penetrating fluid.
Time2-3 hours per side
DifficultyHard

Steps

Preparation

  1. Loosen the wheel bolts, jack the car, support it on stands, remove the front wheel.
  2. Before anything else: if you are reusing the original spring, you need a proper spring compressor. A compressed coil spring stores enough energy to kill someone. If you are not completely confident with a compressor, buy a pre-assembled complete strut assembly instead, it removes this hazard entirely.

Removal

  1. Disconnect anything mounted to the strut body: the sway bar end link, the ABS sensor wire, and the brake line bracket.
  2. Support the control arm with a jack so the suspension doesn't drop when the strut comes free.
  3. Remove the lower strut-to-knuckle pinch bolt or mounting bolts.
  4. Open the hood and remove the upper strut mount nuts in the strut tower, support the strut from below so it doesn't fall as the last nut comes out.
  5. Lower the assembly out.

Installation

  1. If reusing the spring, transfer it with the compressor and fit a new strut mount and bump stop, these wear out and it's false economy to reuse them.
  2. Fit the assembly back up into the tower, start the upper nuts by hand.
  3. Reconnect the lower mount, sway bar link, ABS wire, and brake line bracket.
  4. Torque everything to spec, with the suspension loaded at ride height for anything that passes through a rubber bushing.
  5. Reinstall the wheel, lower the car, get a four-wheel alignment.

Notes

This is the job where people get hurt. A spring compressor slipping under load is genuinely dangerous, so if there's any doubt, complete pre-assembled strut units cost more but remove the risk entirely. Always replace struts in pairs across an axle, a new strut on one side and a worn one on the other makes the car handle unpredictably. Alignment afterward is not optional.
