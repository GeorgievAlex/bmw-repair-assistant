Cabin and engine air filter change

Replace the cabin (pollen) filter and the engine intake air filter, two separate, unrelated filters often done together since both are quick.

TorqueNo meaningful torque specs, these are clip and screw fasteners, hand-tight is correct for essentially all of them.
PartsCabin filter and engine air filter: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN, cabin filters especially have variants (with or without activated charcoal) depending on trim.
ToolsNone beyond basic hand tools, this is one of the easier jobs on the car.
Time15-20 minutes total for both
DifficultyEasy

Steps

Cabin filter

  1. Open the glovebox, remove the side stops/dampers so the glovebox can swing down fully, or remove it entirely depending on your exact trim.
  2. Locate the filter housing behind the glovebox, release the retaining clips or cover.
  3. Slide the old filter out, noting the airflow direction arrow printed on the frame.
  4. Install the new filter in the same orientation, close everything back up.

Engine air filter

  1. Locate the airbox, typically on the side of the engine bay near the front.
  2. Release the clips or screws holding the airbox lid closed.
  3. Lift the lid, remove the old filter, note its orientation.
  4. Wipe out any debris sitting in the bottom of the airbox before installing the new filter.
  5. Reinstall the lid and secure the clips/screws.

Notes

Pay attention to the airflow direction arrow on the cabin filter, installing it backward reduces airflow and defeats some of the filtration. If the cabin filter housing has standing water or visible mold when you open it, check the cowl drain area for a clog, a blocked drain is a common cause and just replacing the filter won't fix the underlying issue.
