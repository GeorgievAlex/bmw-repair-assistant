Symptoms and where to start looking

Works backwards from what you actually notice to which procedure on this site is worth opening first. This narrows where to look, it does not diagnose the car for you, and a symptom can always have a cause that isn't listed here.

Noises

A clunk or knock over bumps, worse on rough roads, is most often front control arm bushings or sway bar end links. Check the end links first, they're far cheaper and quicker to rule out, and a worn one makes a noise well out of proportion to the repair.

A rear-end knock with a vague feeling under throttle or braking points toward rear trailing arm bushings or worn rear shocks.

Squealing that changes with engine speed and not road speed usually means the serpentine belt or one of its pulleys. Spin the tensioner and idler by hand with the belt off and feel for roughness.

A grinding or scraping that changes with road speed and gets louder under braking is brakes, pads worn to the backing plate.

Smells

Sweet smell, like syrup, especially after shutting off, is coolant. Look for weeping at the thermostat housing, water pump, expansion tank seams, and the plastic coolant pipes.

A faint burning smell after the engine's been running a while, with no visible leak on the ground, is commonly valve cover gasket seepage onto hot exhaust components.

Burning smell during or right after hard braking, on new pads, is usually just bedding in and not a fault.

Fluid loss with no puddle

Coolant going down with nothing visible underneath is the classic E90 pattern. Small cracks in the plastic expansion tank or coolant pipes let it escape as steam under pressure and leave nothing to see once cool. Pressure-testing the system finds it faster than looking for drips.

Oil consumption without a puddle is normal to a degree on this engine. Track whether the rate is stable or climbing rather than treating any consumption as a fault.

Electrical

Battery dying repeatedly on an otherwise healthy car is often a battery that was replaced without being registered, so the car keeps charging to the old battery's profile. Check this before buying another battery.

A charging system warning, dimming lights, or voltage that reads wrong at idle points at the alternator, but check the battery and the B+ connection before condemning it, a loose or corroded terminal mimics a failed alternator.

Steering and handling

Play in the steering, a knock when turning slowly, or uneven tyre wear across the tread suggests tie rod ends. Grab the inner rod too, there's no point replacing only the outer if the inner is also worn.

Whining from the power steering that follows engine speed is commonly air in the system or low fluid, not necessarily a failed pump, particularly if it started after any work that opened the system.

The car wandering, pulling, or chewing one edge of a tyre after suspension work almost always means it never got an alignment afterward.

Overheating

Running hot after a coolant change or any cooling system work is most often trapped air that was never properly bled out, not a new fault.

Running hot otherwise, on an engine of this age, puts the electric water pump and thermostat at the top of the list, both are known wear items here.

When nothing here fits

A symptom that doesn't match anything above, or anything that involves brakes failing, steering feeling loose, or the temperature gauge climbing while driving, is worth stopping for rather than working through by elimination. There is no reading-a-webpage version of a safety-critical fault.
