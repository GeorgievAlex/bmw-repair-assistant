E90 325i · N52B25

Brakes · E90 · N52B25

Brake pads and rotors change

Replace brake pads and, when worn or scored past spec, the rotors, front or rear.

TorqueCaliper guide bolts: around 30 Nm. Caliper bracket-to-knuckle bolts: typically higher, in the 100+ Nm range. Wheel bolts: around 120 Nm. Verify all of these against RealOEM or dealer spec for your exact axle and year, front and rear specs differ.
PartsBrake pads, rotors, and anti-rattle clip sets: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN, front and rear are different part numbers and some years changed rotor diameter.
ToolsCaliper piston compression tool (and the electric parking brake retraction tool/procedure if doing the rear), torque wrench, jack and stands.
Time1-1.5 hours per axle
DifficultyMedium

Steps

Preparation

1. Loosen the wheel bolts slightly before jacking, then jack up the car and support it on stands, remove the wheel.

Removal

  1. Remove the caliper guide bolts (or the full caliper mounting bracket bolts if you're also replacing the rotor).
  2. Lift the caliper off and support it with wire, never let it hang by the brake line, that stresses and can damage the hose.
  3. Remove the old pads and anti-rattle clips/shims.
  4. If replacing the rotor: remove the caliper bracket, then the rotor, which may be stuck on by corrosion, a few taps with a rubber mallet usually frees it.
  5. Clean the hub face before installing a new rotor, debris here causes a wobble that feels like a warped rotor later.

Installation

  1. Compress the caliper piston back in with a proper tool, on the rear axle some BMWs use an electric parking brake caliper that needs to be wound back with a special tool or retracted electronically, do not just push it like a front piston.
  2. Install new pads and clips, reinstall caliper and bracket, torque everything to spec.
  3. Pump the brake pedal firmly several times before driving to seat the pads against the rotor.

Notes

Reset the brake pad wear sensor if your car has one, or the dashboard warning light stays on even with fresh pads. Bed in new pads with a series of gentle stops from moderate speed before relying on hard braking, this transfers an even layer of pad material onto the rotor and meaningfully affects how well they work afterward. If the rear uses an electric parking brake, do not attempt to compress that piston with a standard tool, you can damage the motor.
