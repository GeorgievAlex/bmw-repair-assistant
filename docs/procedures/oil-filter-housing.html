Oil filter housing replacement

Replace the oil filter housing itself, not just the filter element inside it. This is a different, bigger job than a routine oil change, done when the housing has cracked or is leaking from somewhere other than the cap O-rings.

TorqueHousing mounting bolts: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec for your exact engine revision, this varies by whether your housing uses a separate gasket or an integrated seal design.
PartsOil filter housing assembly and mounting gasket/seal: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN, there have been revised versions of this housing across the production run addressing earlier cracking issues, worth checking if a superseded part number applies to your car.
ToolsStandard socket set, drain pan, possibly extensions/universal joints depending on access clearance.
Time2-3 hours depending on what needs to come off for access
DifficultyMedium-Hard

Steps

Preparation

  1. Drain the oil first, same as for a routine oil change, there's no way to remove the housing with oil still in the system.
  2. Remove whatever is in the way for access, on this engine that commonly includes intake ducting and sometimes the alternator or its bracket, depending on exactly how the housing is oriented on your specific engine bay layout.

Removal

  1. Disconnect the oil pressure/level sensor connectors if they're mounted on the housing.
  2. Remove the housing mounting bolts, noting their positions, they're not all identical length on some versions.
  3. Lift the housing away from the block, have a drain pan ready, there's always residual oil that comes with it.
  4. Clean the mating surface on the block thoroughly.

Installation

  1. Install the new housing with a new gasket/seal, torque the mounting bolts evenly in a crisscross pattern.
  2. Reconnect sensors and reinstall anything removed for access, refill with oil and a new filter element, check for leaks once running.

Notes

If you're replacing the housing because the old one cracked, inspect it once it's out, cracking at the same spot repeatedly across many owners' cars is a known pattern on this engine family and is worth researching before you reinstall the exact same design if a revised part number exists. Don't skip cleaning the mating surface, a rushed cleanup here is the most common reason for a comeback leak.
