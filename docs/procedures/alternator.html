Electrical · E90 · N52B25

Alternator replacement

Replace a failed or failing alternator.

TorqueAlternator mounting bolts: commonly cited as 10 Nm plus an additional 180 degree turn, consistent with a torque-to-yield, one-time-use aluminum bolt design, do not reuse these bolts. B+ terminal nut: verify against RealOEM or dealer spec. Confirm both against your exact year before relying on these numbers.
PartsAlternator, and the mounting bolts if they're the one-time-use type for your car: verify exact OEM part numbers against RealOEM.com using your VIN. Also confirm whether your alternator has an integrated voltage regulator before ordering a replacement, this varies.
ToolsSocket set, torque wrench capable of angle-based tightening if your bolts use the torque-to-yield spec, possibly need to loosen other components for clearance depending on your engine bay layout.
Time1.5-2.5 hours
DifficultyMedium-Hard

Steps

Preparation

  1. Disconnect the battery negative terminal first, this is a live electrical component.
  2. Remove the serpentine belt (see the serpentine belt procedure).

Removal

  1. Disconnect the electrical connector and the B+ terminal nut from the alternator, note the orientation before you pull anything off.
  2. Remove the alternator mounting bolts, these are typically one-time-use torque-to-yield bolts on this engine, buy new ones before you start, don't plan to reuse the old ones.
  3. Maneuver the alternator out, it can be a tight fit depending on what else is routed through the engine bay on your specific car.

Installation

  1. Install the new or rebuilt alternator in reverse order, using new mounting bolts, torque them to spec in the correct stages.
  2. Torque the B+ terminal nut to spec, reinstall the belt, reconnect the battery.
  3. Start the engine and verify charging voltage is correct before considering the job done.

Notes

Always disconnect the battery first. Double check the B+ terminal nut is properly torqued, a loose one can arc and melt the connector, which turns a straightforward job into an expensive one. If charging voltage still looks wrong after the swap, check the battery and wiring before assuming the new alternator is also bad, it's an easy trap to fall into.
